Verdict
Recommended

Scream is one of those horror movies that just doesn’t get its due from “true” horror fans, because it is so mainstream. Wes Craven knows the slasher rules cold, puts them in the characters’ mouths, and then keeps moving the floor. I have watched it enough times to know the shape of the night. The twists and turns still get me.

Too famous to be taken seriously

A lot of horror people file this one under popcorn and move on, like popularity disqualified it. The jokes, the mask, the way Woodsboro talks about scary movies out loud — all of that made Scream huge, and huge made it easy to shrug off. That shrug misses the movie. Sidney Prescott is not a punchline, and Craven is as mean about breaking the rules as he is about naming them. Mainstream doesn’t mean soft. It means the scare traveled, and some fans never forgave it for that.

The turns still get me

Knowing where it goes doesn’t flatten it. Every rewatch I catch a look I talked over last time, a line that was doing two jobs, a cut that arrives a beat earlier than my memory. The mystery keeps its teeth because the movie is built on misdirection, not one door at the end. I walk in ready and still get turned around. That’s the rare trick: a horror film this famous that still plays like it has something left to spring.

Night two on the scare schedule

Final verdict — 8 / 10 popcorn buckets

Overall, I recommend Scream. It doesn’t get its due from the crowd that writes off anything this mainstream, and the twists and turns still get me on every rewatch. An easy yes if you want a slasher that knows the genre and still has the nerve to surprise you.
